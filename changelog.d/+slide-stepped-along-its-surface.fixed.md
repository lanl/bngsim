- **A slide along a rate-law switch's surface took a number of steps that grew as 1/rtol, and at a tight tolerance did not finish.** Where a switch's two branches each push the state onto its surface, the state slides along it. Issue #908 held such a slide's crossings off the root search, but its rate law still turned on every side of the surface a Newton iterate landed on, and the steps shrank to the tolerance's own scale. A three-species clock sweep took 34,000 steps at rtol 1e-3, 34 million (91 s) at 1e-6, and did not finish at the default of 1e-8. A threshold that reads `time()` chattered the same way. A slide is now stepped as Filippov's sliding mode, the limit that chatter converges to: the blend of the two branches that keeps the state on its surface. It ends where one side's flow stops carrying the state onto the surface, and the step over that end is taken again up to it. A slide is found whether its crossings are declined, restarted, or never reach a step's end. The clock sweep now takes a few hundred steps at any tolerance and matches its closed form.

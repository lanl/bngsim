- **A rate-law switch on a steep threshold, crossed where the threshold was steepest, could lose the window the crossing opens, with nothing said.** To restart at a crossing, a run asks that its own trajectory go past the surface by more than the tolerance can blur, over the rest of the step. That blur was worked out from the threshold's slope at the crossing, so a crossing in the steep of a sweep set a bar taller than the sweep itself. Then no point past it could clear the bar, even where the threshold had flattened out. In #908's clock sweep, a threshold that rises by 0.5 on a clock species, with the state drifting at 0.05 or 0.1, the run stepped on over a pulse the crossing lets through: Y gained 0 where the pulse gives it 0.0068 and 0.0032. Where the threshold is smooth, the blur is now worked out from the slope at each point the trajectory is read at. A switch whose residual is linear in its species, such as `S < thr` for a constant thr, decides as before. So does one that jumps at the crossing, such as a Boolean network's condition on one of its own `if()` functions.

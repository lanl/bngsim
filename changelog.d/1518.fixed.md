- **`steady_states()` merged distinct roots closer than `tol` in concentration, and dropped one of a nanomolar switch's three ([#1518](https://github.com/lanl/bngsim/issues/1518)).** The clustering took `tol`, a residual tolerance in concentration per time, as an absolute merge distance in concentration. In molar units at nanomolar scale the Schlogl switch's roots at 0.2 nM and 0.5 nM were one root, the seed decided whether the attractor or the saddle survived, and nothing warned; the same model at unit scale gave all three. The floor is now a distance in concentration: for each species, `1e-9` of the largest value it takes at any converged start, or double precision's resolution beside the largest magnitude the solves saw, whichever is larger, beside the law floor that was already there; `SteadyStates.cluster(atol=None)` derives it, and a given `atol` is used as given. `tol` plays no part. A start KINSOL leaves short of its root (Newton distance above 1), accepted under `tol`, is now solved once more from where it stopped: without `tol` as the floor it would come back as a root of its own.
